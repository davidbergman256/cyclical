# Analysis: Observation-based task mining / "work graph" vendors (Skan AI, Soroco, Mimica, KYP.ai) and record-to-agent startups (Sola, Orby/Uniphore, Scribe Optimize, Encore)

These vendors put a sensor on every employee's desktop, record clicks, keystrokes, app switches and screen content, abstract that into a map of how work flows (Soroco literally calls it a "work graph"), and since 2025 turn the map into agents. They are the most literal match to Cyclical's A2 (observe silently, build the graph) and are moving into A1 (graph with people and agents in it) and A9 (recommend redesigns), but none of them grades anything against what happens in the world: their "value" metric is effort saved (time, clicks, cost per transaction), not payments or show-ups. **Overlap score: 4/10** — same front half of the loop (discover → graph → agent), almost nothing of the back half (outcome-graded shadow/checkoff/graduation, pooled outcomes), and an opposite customer (Fortune 50 banks and insurers, not 9-person clinics). The single most important lesson: Skan's CEO argues that system logs "miss the human work behind completed transactions" — which is a direct hit on Cyclical's log-only A2, and in dental it is partly right (the re-typing between systems, the phone call before the booking) — but the vendors' answer, screen surveillance, is something a small clinic cannot get through its own staff, so Cyclical needs a third answer, not theirs.

## What it actually is

**Task mining** is the desktop-side cousin of process mining. Process mining (Celonis's method) reconstructs a process from the event logs of systems of record — "invoice created", "invoice approved" timestamps in SAP. Task mining puts an agent on the employee's PC and records what happens *between* those log entries: which windows they switch through, what they copy from one app into another, how long each screen takes. Analysts have renamed the category "digital interaction intelligence" (Everest Group) and Gartner publishes a "Market Guide for Task Mining Tools" (2026 edition lists KYP.ai) [8][3].

**Architecture common to all of them:** (1) a desktop or browser agent captures UI events and screenshots, scoped to whitelisted apps/URLs; (2) on-device OCR/computer vision abstracts screens into actions ("opened claim #, copied policy number, pasted into CRM"); (3) PII is masked and data aggregated across many workers so no individual is profiled; (4) a clustering/sequence model groups actions into tasks and variants, which become the map/graph; (5) since 2025, the map plus recorded demonstrations seed a computer-use agent or generated RPA/agent code.

**A2 is where the real argument is.** Skan's CEO pitch: system logs miss the human work behind completed transactions; the hard problem is "abstraction of what you see on the screen" [2]. Cyclical's realizations already found that in a 9-person clinic 12 of 14 hand-typed handoffs end in the practice software — so the *destination* of most handoffs is logged, but the *source* (the insurer portal screen, the paper form, the phone call) often is not. A log-only approach sees "appointment created at 10:42" but not "front desk spent 6 minutes on hold with Delta Dental first." Desktop observation catches the portal screen and the re-typing; neither approach catches the phone call or the chairside conversation, and in a dental front office those are a large share of the work. What task mining also misses entirely: *why* someone did something, any work off the instrumented PC (phone, mobile, paper, in-person), and — critically for Cyclical — the downstream result. A screenshot of a submitted claim does not tell you whether it was paid.

**A5/A12 is where Cyclical is clearly different.** Everything these vendors measure is effort: context switches, handling time, clicks, cost per transaction. Skan's agents copy "what top performers do," where top means fastest. That is the "minimize difference from the human" stage-1 design that thinking.md rejects, and it Goodharts in the way realizations §5 predicts (a faster claims clerk who downcodes looks like a top performer). None joins observed work to outcomes in the system of record (claim paid, patient showed). Encore AI is the only adjacent player found that selects behavior by outcome ("which approaches worked"), and it works on calls, not process graphs.

**A9 is where they are slightly ahead.** Avali's "simulate before changing anything in the real world" is the same move as Cyclical's orchestrator proposing reorganizations, with a simulator Cyclical has only as a prototype (the dental flow simulator). But simulation calibrated on effort data predicts effort, not outcomes; it can't answer "can this role be removed without hurting collections," which needs the randomized pooled experiment of A11.

## Where they're ahead of Cyclical

- **Seeing work that leaves no log.** Desktop capture sees legacy screens, portal lookups and copy-paste re-keying. For a dental clinic, the insurer-portal eligibility check and the copy into the PMS are exactly the "about a fifth of handoffs are a person re-typing" that Cyclical wants to remove first — and the event log of the PMS only sees the paste.
- **Evidence that the graph helps agents.** Soroco's vendor test: agents with credentials only passed 0%, with a written SOP ~40%, with the full work graph 82% [4][5]. Vendor-run and unaudited, but it is the only quantitative claim found that "context from observed work" lifts agent success, which supports Cyclical's A10.
- **Works-council playbook.** Skan claims European works-council approvals (Allianz) via aggregation across hundreds of workers, opt-in app scoping, on-device processing and data inside the firewall [2]. That is a tested privacy design Cyclical can borrow.
- **Distribution and validation.** Seven years, ~$120M (Skan), Fortune 50 logos, five years of analyst #1 rankings (Soroco). They have already been through security reviews and procurement Cyclical will never face in SMB, but which a DSO buyer may demand.
- **Simulation of redesigns (Avali)** before rollout.

## Where they're doing it differently (and whether their way is better)

- **Screens vs logs (A2).** Theirs is better at coverage, worse at consent and cost. For an enterprise with 1,500 finance staff, a COE and a works council, aggregation across hundreds of workers makes individual surveillance implausible. In a 9-person clinic aggregation is impossible: there is one biller, so any "pattern" is that person's performance record. Their privacy design does not transfer down-market. Cyclical's log-based approach is the right default for SMB; the gap is the portal/re-typing work, which can be closed narrowly (e.g. a browser extension scoped to insurer portals, or read access to the portals' own logs) rather than with full-desktop recording. Rough probability that full desktop capture is acceptable to small-clinic staff at scale: ~20%.
- **Imitation vs outcome grading (A5).** Their way is worse for Cyclical's goal: copying top performers caps the agent at the best human and imports their shortcuts. It is cheaper and faster to show value (no waiting weeks for insurer remittances), which is why enterprises buy it.
- **One-shot agentification vs staged graduation (A7/A8).** They hand discovered tasks to an agent or RPA bot and move on; Cyclical keeps the human in shadow/checkoff until a statistical bar is met per case type. Theirs gets to a demo faster; Cyclical's is the only one with an answer to "how do you know it's safe to remove the human."
- **Pooling behavior vs pooling outcomes (A11).** Soroco's TribeScope pools interaction data across customers (as far as can be told) to understand *how software is used*. That pool improves discovery accuracy for every customer and is a real moat in discovery. It cannot run outcome A/B tests, certify rare segments, or test whether a role can be removed, because it has no outcomes.

## Feedback & reviews on these parts of the loop

**Surveillance / privacy (A2)** — independent commentary:
- Commentators on Skan's raise: continuous observation of employee screens "can be perceived as surveillance"; the data source is "meaningfully different (and more invasive) than process-mining logs" and the company "hasn't addressed head-on the monitoring questions" (search extracts of secondary coverage) [2][13].
- One headline framed the raise as "Skan AI Raises $63M for Employee Work Monitoring" [13]; TNW's headline: "Skan AI raises $63m to watch how office staff actually work, then build agents that copy them" [14]. The press framing is surveillance-plus-replacement, which is the reputational risk Cyclical inherits if it ever records screens.
- The Allianz works-council approval is Skan's account, not Allianz's, and coverage juxtaposed it with Allianz's separately announced 1,800 job cuts [2]. This is the "staff are labeling their own replacement" problem from realizations §5, visible in the press.
- Legal context (from memory): in Germany, any technical system "suitable" to monitor employee behavior or performance requires works-council co-determination (BetrVG §87(1) no. 6), and under GDPR a data-protection impact assessment (Art. 35) is expected for systematic monitoring. This is why European task-mining deployments are slow and why vendors stress anonymization, opt-in and on-device processing [3][2].

**Accuracy of discovered graphs** — almost entirely vendor-sourced:
- Soroco's 0% / ~40% / 82% agent pass-rate tiers (vendor test, no methodology published) [4][5].
- Skan: 30–40% average operational savings, ">$500M cumulative customer value," "32% lower cost per transaction" for its agents — company-reported, no named auditor; one outlet notes claimed AI productivity gains "have a poor record under inspection" [1][13].
- Scribe's own comparison concedes Optimize "needs captured workflows to analyze, and is not an automation tool by itself" [11] — i.e. discovery alone does not close the loop.
- Independent accuracy studies of task-mining maps: none found in this session.

**Time-to-value** — not retrievable: Gartner Peer Insights has a Scout review page [3] but scores and review counts could not be fetched; no G2/Reddit evidence was retrieved. From memory, typical task-mining pilots run 4–12 weeks of capture before a usable map, after security review and (in Europe) works-council negotiation; treat as unverified.

**Analyst signal:** Everest's DII PEAK Matrix 2026 assessed 17 providers [3]; Gartner's 2026 Task Mining Market Guide exists [7]. A "Market Guide" (not a Magic Quadrant) usually means Gartner still sees the category as immature (from memory on Gartner's conventions).

## Top-level: why they chose this path, and what to learn

**Starting asset and constraint.** These companies were born in the 2016–2020 RPA wave (UiPath, Automation Anywhere). Their buyer was the enterprise "automation center of excellence" whose problem was "which of our 10,000 clerical tasks should we build bots for?" Enterprise systems of record (SAP, mainframes, Citrix-wrapped legacy apps) either produce no useful logs or are owned by Celonis-style process miners, so the open niche was the desktop. Their whole stack — screen capture, computer vision on legacy UIs, PII masking, aggregation across hundreds of workers — is shaped by large, regulated, log-poor, unionized-in-Europe customers. The 2025–26 "agents" pivot is the same asset re-labeled: the RPA bot became a computer-use agent, and the process map became "the context layer AI agents can't see." Their incentive is to sell discovery and effort savings fast; outcome grading would slow every deal by weeks of waiting for downstream results and would require deep integration with systems they don't own.

**Is Cyclical a clone, subset, superset or orthogonal?** Superset on the loop, orthogonal on market and sensor. Cyclical contains their front half (observe → graph → agent → recommend redesign) and adds the half they lack (outcome-graded shadow/checkoff/graduation, anti-Goodhart guardrails, pooled outcome experiments). It differs on sensor (system logs vs screens) and customer (one vertical of small businesses vs multi-vertical enterprise). Evidence: none of the four publishes any outcome-based evaluation, staged graduation or cross-customer experiment; all sell to Fortune-500-scale buyers.

**Copy:**
- The work graph as agent context (A10), with the 0%/40%/82% framing as a testable internal benchmark: measure Cyclical's agents with and without the graph.
- Their privacy primitives where they fit: app/URL whitelists, on-device abstraction, only metadata leaves the machine.
- Variant discovery (the same task done several ways) as the raw material for A7's case types.
- A redesign simulator (Avali) in front of the orchestrator's proposals — but calibrated on outcomes.

**Avoid:**
- Full-desktop screen recording as the default sensor in SMB: no aggregation possible, so it is individual surveillance, and it is the headline the press already writes ("watch how office staff work, then build agents that copy them").
- "Copy the top performer" as the training signal (A5); it is imitation with a ceiling, and fastest ≠ best outcome.
- Effort metrics (handle time, context switches) as the headline value; they Goodhart.

**Exploit:**
- Their structural blind spot is outcomes. A pitch of "we grade on whether the claim got paid, not how fast it was typed" is something none of them can match without integrating with systems of record and waiting weeks per data point.
- They cannot go down-market: their privacy model depends on hundreds of workers per role, their sales motion on six-figure deals and a COE. Small clinics are unserved by design.
- Narrow observation for the log gap: rather than screen-record, capture only the specific log-poor surfaces (insurer portals, clearinghouse sites) with a scoped browser extension, which is closer to Scribe's free-extension model than to Skan's.

**Threat level: low now (~2/10), medium if one of three things happens.** The observations that would tell David the threat is rising:
1. A task-mining vendor joins observed work to system-of-record outcomes (e.g. Skan Intelligence reporting "claims paid" or "denial rate by task variant" rather than handling time) — that would be them building A5/A12.
2. Soroco or Skan launches a vertical healthcare/dental-RCM package or partners with a DSO or practice-management vendor (Dentrix/Henry Schein One, Open Dental) — DSOs are enterprise-shaped and are exactly where their sales motion reaches dental.
3. Scribe Optimize (free extension, small-team footprint, $1.3B valuation) adds agent generation plus any outcome tracking — Scribe is the one player in this cluster with bottom-up SMB distribution.

**Other incumbents found that may be close to Cyclical:** Encore AI ($30M Series A, July 2026 — learns which approaches *worked* from call outcomes and turns them into agents; the only outcome-selected behavior seen in this cluster) [12]; Scribe Optimize (SMB-reachable workflow mining) [11]; Sola (record-once → agent) [9]; Uniphore (after buying Orby) [10]; Markov (sells screen recordings with mouse/keyboard traces to frontier labs — a sign labs will train general computer-use skills on this data, which commoditizes A8 level 1) [15]. Basepilot was on the list but could not be researched (search budget exhausted).
