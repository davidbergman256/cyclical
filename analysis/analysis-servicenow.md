# Analysis: ServiceNow

ServiceNow is the clearest live example of the strategic risk named in realizations.md §6: the company that owns the workflow system of record (for enterprise IT, HR and customer service) has built the orchestration layer on top of it. It now ships flows in which a step can be done by a person or an AI agent, an agent coordinator, a governance console that inventories every agent and blocks the ones acting outside their permissions, and an identity-permissions graph bought for about $1B. **Overlap score: 4/10.** The frame (A1), the shared context (A10) and the governance plumbing (A4, part of A12) overlap heavily. The core of Cyclical's loop does not: grading against real-world outcomes, seeded-error checkoffs, statistical graduation per case type, and outcome pooling across customers (A5–A7, A11). ServiceNow grades agents with an LLM judge reading execution logs. Its only cross-customer pooling feeds model training with masked prompts. **The main lesson is that the system-of-record owner charged for control first.** Inventory, permissions and kill switches are what enterprises paid a 20–30%+ uplift for. ServiceNow has not built evaluation against outcomes, and in IT and HR work it mostly can't, because the "outcome" there is a ticket state that its own platform writes.

## What it actually is

**Business.** ServiceNow sells the Now Platform, the workflow system of record for large enterprises. Every IT incident, HR case, customer-service case, change request and asset lives as a row in its task tables, alongside the CMDB (configuration management database, the map of the company's IT assets and how they depend on each other). Q3 2026 subscription revenue was $3.99B, up 21% year over year. 680 customers pay more than $5M a year in ACV (annual contract value), and 31 more passed $20M ACV over the past year [1][3]. Its customer base is about 8,000+ mostly large enterprises (from memory), and it has no small-business product.

**AI products and how they're layered:**

- **Now Assist** (since late 2023) is generative-AI "skills" such as summarizing cases, drafting resolution notes, chat and code generation. It's sold as a "Pro Plus" tier on top of the Pro seat. The default model is ServiceNow's own Now LLM; other models plug in through a Generative AI Controller (from memory).
- **AI Agents, AI Agent Studio and AI Agent Orchestrator** (2024–25) let customers build agents in AI Agent Studio. The Orchestrator coordinates several agents on one task, and the Flow Designer action "Use an AI agent" drops an agent into a deterministic flow as one step [5][6].
- **AI Agent Fabric** (2025) is the agent-to-agent and MCP (Model Context Protocol, the standard for connecting agents to tools) layer for third-party agents (from memory).
- **AI Control Tower**, which ServiceNow's own Q3 2026 release now calls the "Super Intelligence Control Tower" [1], is organized around five pillars: discover, observe, govern, secure, measure. It inventories models, agents, prompts, datasets and MCP servers, including ones running in AWS, Azure, GCP, SAP, Oracle and Workday (30 new integrations at Knowledge 2026). It runs risk assessments aligned to the EU AI Act and NIST, puts approval gates on activating an MCP server, tracks cost and ROI at the token and workflow level, and shuts an agent down if it acts outside its mapped permissions [12][13][14][15]. Control Tower net new ACV grew more than 6× year over year in Q3 2026 [1].
- **Workflow Data Fabric and RaptorDB** (2024–) are the data layer. "Zero-copy" connectors query Snowflake, Databricks and similar stores without copying the data, and RaptorDB is ServiceNow's faster Postgres-derived database for large instances (from memory).
- **Process Mining** (since about 2022) reconstructs process maps from ServiceNow's own task-table audit history, and only from that history (from memory).
- **Acquisitions:** Moveworks ($2.85B, closed 15 Dec 2025) is a conversational front door in Slack and Teams with enterprise search. About 250 customers already used both products; the combined products reach 5.5M employee users; nearly 90% of Moveworks customers deploy it to 100% of their staff [9][10][11]. Veza (about $1B, closed 2 Mar 2026) is an identity-security platform for human and non-human identities, including AI agents [16][17][18][19][20]. Armis (cyber-asset security, about $7.75B, announced Dec 2025) is from memory.

**Traction.** AI net new ACV grew 65% year over year in Q3 2026. AI deals over $1M grew 4.5×. The year-end AI ACV target was raised from $1B at the start of 2026 to at least $1.7B [1][2][4]. Agentic-AI consumption in production was reported up more than 13× year to date [2]. The stock still fell after hours on Q3, on an EPS miss and seat-compression worries [1][4]. No source gave a Pro Plus customer count.

**Pricing.** List prices are not published. On the Q2 2026 call management cited a 20–30% price uplift for AI-native products and more than 30% for Pro Plus [2]. Third-party advisors estimate 40–60% over Pro per fulfiller ("fulfiller" is ServiceNow's term for a licensed worker who resolves tickets) [21][22]. About half of net new business is now not seat-based, under a hybrid seat-plus-consumption model where usage is metered in "assists" [2]. One advisor reports that from April 2026 Now Assist is bundled into new ITSM tiers (Foundation, Advanced, Prime), which would make the older uplift figures outdated. Sources conflict on this [21].

### The aspects that matter

**A1: same graph, no promotion mechanism.** ServiceNow already treats a business process as a graph of steps with typed handoffs. A flow step can be a human task assigned to a group or an AI-agent action. Its own community guidance names two patterns: "agent-driven", where the agent decides when to ask a human, and "workflow-driven", where the flow fixes where people take part [5]. The marketing line "AI agents as part of the workforce" is close to Cyclical's interchangeable occupant. The difference is who moves the occupant. In ServiceNow, a platform admin rewires the flow. In Cyclical, evidence (outcome-graded shadow runs leading to graduation) moves it. ServiceNow has every building block except the evidence engine that decides when a step should change hands.

**A4: permissions. ServiceNow is ahead on half of this and has publicly shown why Cyclical's other half matters.** With Veza, ServiceNow can do what thinking.md asks for under "unused permissions → proposed removal": an access graph across people, service accounts and agents, with over-privilege and unused-access detection, plus a Control Tower that kills an agent acting outside policy [16][19][14]. KuppingerCole's assessment: Veza "strengthens visibility, but it does not eliminate the governance gap" [18]. It has no node-to-node just-in-time request protocol, and it doesn't turn repeated requests into missing-edge proposals. The more useful evidence is the failure case (from memory, about 80% confident on details). In late 2025, AppOmni researchers showed "second-order prompt injection" in Now Assist AI agents. Under default settings, agents could discover and recruit each other, so text a low-privilege user planted in a record field could get a higher-privilege agent to copy data or change records through another agent. ServiceNow's response was that the system behaved as designed, plus configuration guidance. A separate critical flaw in the Virtual Agent / AI Agents API ("BodySnatcher", disclosed January 2026, from memory) allowed user impersonation. This is exactly the attack thinking.md designs against: agent-to-agent approvals have to be decided by who is asking, what class of data and what kind of task, never by the wording of the request. ServiceNow's main injection defense, Guardian, classifies wording. Cyclical's rule is the better design, and ServiceNow's incidents are the case for it.

**A4, unresolved question: who answers for a system node?** ServiceNow's answer is implicit but complete. The system of record *is* the authority, and every table has ACLs owned by admins. An agent's request for data is just an ACL check under the identity it runs as. That works because ServiceNow owns the system. Cyclical won't own Dentrix or Eaglesoft, so it can't copy this. The nearest transferable idea is Veza's: model the system's own permission graph as data, rather than assigning a "responsible person" node.

**A5–A7: evaluation. This is where Cyclical is clearly differentiated.** Agentic Evaluations is an LLM judge, the Now LLM, scoring execution logs on task completeness (1–3), tool-call correctness and tool choice (Excellent/Moderate/Poor), over 50–100+ runs it generates itself [7][8]. ServiceNow's own field guides warn it is "decision support, not a deployment gate," that the scores are "probabilistic", and that it should run only in sub-production because evaluations execute real workflows [7][8]. Nothing scores the agent, or the human, against what happened afterwards: whether the incident reopened, whether the employee's problem was actually solved, whether the customer churned. Checkoff is an approval gate with no audit of the approver. Graduation is a toggle plus a configurable confidence threshold. The reason is structural, not an oversight. In ITSM and HR, the strongest answer key is a ticket state the platform writes itself, and closure is not proof of resolution. Cyclical's verticals have external answer keys: insurer remittances, bank deposits, show-ups. This is the main thing ServiceNow cannot copy simply by shipping a feature.

**A11: pooling. ServiceNow does pool, but inputs, not outcomes.** The brief asked for verification. Now Assist has a data-sharing program in which masked and anonymized prompts and responses from opted-in instances are used to build synthetic training data for Now LLMs [23][24][25]. It behaves like a default-on program. Collection begins 30 days after an advanced AI product is installed. Opting out takes up to 5 business days, doesn't remove data already collected, and opting back in requires contacting your account executive or support [23][25]. Classic Virtual Agent NLU training stays inside each instance [24]. Separately, from memory, ServiceNow has long offered opt-in Performance Analytics Benchmarks that compare ITSM KPIs such as resolution time across anonymized peers. That is pooled outcome *description*, not pooled experimentation. ServiceNow therefore pools to make the shared skill better (Cyclical's replacement level 1). It does not pool to grade, certify or run randomized tests on removing a role. It probably never will: its customers are large, heterogeneous enterprises that run their own customized processes, and they would treat cross-customer experiments as a contractual and political nonstarter.

**A9: orchestrator. Same name, different job.** "AI Agent Orchestrator" is a runtime coordinator: it picks which agent handles which sub-task within a conversation or case. It is not a manager-facing agent that says "merge these three steps; this role can go." Process Mining plus Control Tower "Measure" supplies the raw inputs for such proposals, but people turn them into reorganizations. The firms doing that are mostly consulting partners (Accenture, Deloitte), not the product.

## Where they're ahead of Cyclical

- **Governance plumbing that buyers already pay for.** This covers agent and model inventory across vendors, risk intake forms, MCP-server approval gates, an identity graph over humans and agents, unused-access detection, and a kill switch on out-of-policy actions [12][14][15][16]. Control Tower ACV up more than 6× is the market signal that "control" sells before "improvement" [1].
- **Shared context (A10).** The CMDB, knowledge bases and zero-copy data fabric give every agent business-wide context, after 20 years of building the data model.
- **Distribution into "where people already work" (A3).** Moveworks puts the agent in Slack and Teams, with 5.5M combined employee users and about 90% of Moveworks customers on full-company deployment [9][11]. That is Cyclical's "no new box" principle executed at scale, in the chat client rather than the email drafts folder.
- **Deterministic level first (A8).** ServiceNow's history is level 3: deterministic flows, assignment rules, SLAs. Agents are added only where rules ran out. That's a sound reminder that much of a small business's process may be rules from day one, not something the agent graduates into.

## Where they're doing it differently (and whether their way is better)

- **Mining inside their own walls versus mining everyone's logs (A2).** ServiceNow mines its own tables and pushes customers to move work into the platform, which is the sales motion. Cyclical reads email, calendar, practice software and accounting. For a 9-person dental clinic, ServiceNow's way is impossible: the clinic's work does not live in one platform, and the practice-management vendor won't hand over its tables. Cyclical's way is better for its target. ServiceNow's way is better for data quality where it applies.
- **Admin-decided occupancy versus evidence-decided occupancy (A1, A7).** ServiceNow lets the customer's platform team decide when an agent takes a step. That suits enterprises with platform teams and change-advisory boards, and it fails for a clinic with no admin. Cyclical's automatic graduation is the right design for buyers who have no platform team.
- **Seat uplift versus outcome-linked value (A14).** ServiceNow monetizes AI as a 20–30%+ price uplift plus consumption units [2]. That model is exposed to seat compression: if agents cut the number of fulfillers, the seat base shrinks, and the after-hours drop on Q3 2026 shows investors already price that risk [1][4]. Cyclical's gross margin comes from compute savings on a per-clinic bill, so model releases help it rather than hurt.
- **Governing agents versus grading them (A5–A7, A12).** ServiceNow's "Measure" pillar connects spend to ROI dashboards. That's an accounting view, not a guardrail paired with each local eval. Cyclical's pairing (claims acceptance against collections) is more rigorous, but it needs external outcomes that IT and HR work mostly lacks.

## Feedback & reviews on these parts of the loop

**Evaluation (A5–A7), vendor-adjacent but self-critical.** ServiceNow's own Center-of-Excellence field guides call Agentic Evaluations "decision support, not a deployment gate on its own," with scores that are "AI-generated and probabilistic," and advise running them "only in sub-production" because they execute real workflows [7][8]. I found no published validation of the LLM judge.

**Pricing and packaging (A14), independent advisors.** Licensing advisors report that ServiceNow quoted Now Assist "as a firm line" with paid pilots offered only on request. Account teams "frequently present Pro Plus as a platform tier rather than an add-on," which makes it look as though every fulfiller needs Pro Plus to get any AI. Because Now Assist is bundled rather than metered separately, buyers "cannot cleanly compare 'AI cost' across deployments" [21][22][26]. Uplift estimates range from 25–30% to 50–60% over base tiers, and advisors compare this unfavourably with Freshservice and Jira Service Management, which include AI in base pricing [21][22]. Management's own figure is 20–30%+ (vendor, [2]).

**Permissions and governance (A4), independent analysts.** KuppingerCole calls Veza ServiceNow's "first serious entry" into identity governance but says it "does not eliminate the governance gap" [18]. Diginomica frames identity governance as "the critical bottleneck" in moving enterprise AI from pilot to production [19]. The AppOmni prompt-injection research and the BodySnatcher disclosure (from memory, see A4 above) are the strongest independent evidence that agent-to-agent trust is where these platforms break.

**Adoption (A14), vendor numbers only.** These are 65% AI net-new-ACV growth, 4.5× more $1M+ AI deals, 13× agentic consumption, and a target of $1.7B AI ACV (vendor, [1][2][4]). One call excerpt says customers who got deployment support adopted AI "twice as fast" in the first five weeks of that support [2]. That implies adoption without hands-on help is slow, consistent with "the months practices need to trust each step" in realizations.md. Fetch limits prevented me from collecting peer-review scores and Reddit threads.

**Data sharing (A11), community.** Admins on the ServiceNow Community ask repeatedly what opting out breaks. The answer is that all Now Assist features keep working, but data already collected isn't removed and opting back in needs the account team [23][25]. I found no public controversy (about 70% confident none exists at scale).

## Top-level: why they chose this path, and what to learn

**Starting asset and constraint.** ServiceNow owns the rows: every ticket, approval, SLA and asset record for 8k+ large enterprises, plus an admin community that already builds flows. That made three things cheap: putting agents in as flow steps (A1), giving them context (A10), and selling governance to CIOs who were afraid of agent sprawl. It made two things hard. First, the outcomes that matter (was the employee actually unblocked, did the customer stay) mostly happen outside the ticket, so evaluation fell back on an LLM judge. Second, cross-customer outcome experiments are contractually and culturally impossible with Fortune 500 buyers who each customize their processes. The economic incentive to graduate agents automatically is also mixed, because ServiceNow is paid per fulfiller seat and every graduated case type is a candidate seat cut. Consumption pricing is the hedge [2].

**What this means for Cyclical:**

- **Copy:** Treat governance as a feature the buyer can see, not just internal plumbing. A one-page inventory for the clinic owner (which agents exist, what each can touch, what they did last week, a kill switch) is what ServiceNow proved buyers pay for. Copy Veza's idea for the unresolved system-node question too: read the practice software's own permission model as data, instead of inventing a "responsible" person node.
- **Avoid:** Don't grade with an LLM judge as the primary signal; it is ServiceNow's weakest point and Cyclical's main differentiator. Don't let agents discover and recruit each other by default. Don't price as a seat uplift that your own success erodes.
- **Exploit:** ServiceNow cannot go down-market to a 9-person clinic. It has no small-business product, needs an admin per instance, and sells $5M+ ACV deals [3]. It also cannot pool outcomes across customers. The real dental threat is a practice-software vendor running ServiceNow's playbook: Henry Schein One (Dentrix), Patterson (Eaglesoft), Planet DDS (Denticon) or Curve.

**Is Cyclical a clone, subset or superset?** Cyclical overlaps ServiceNow's frame and governance layers, adds an evaluation-and-pooling engine ServiceNow lacks, and targets a market ServiceNow does not serve. The best description is a superset of the loop and orthogonal on market. Cyclical is not a clone: the evidence is the absence of anything outcome-graded, any seeded-error audit or any per-case-type graduation in ServiceNow's 2026 releases.

**Threat level: low directly (about 10% that ServiceNow competes in small dental practices within 3 years), but high as a template.** These observations would show the threat rising:

1. ServiceNow ships outcome-linked evaluation, for example grading agents on reopen rate or CSAT instead of LLM-judge completeness, or uses Benchmarks to certify agents across customers.
2. ServiceNow launches a packaged mid-market or vertical healthcare-provider revenue-cycle edition.
3. Most directly, a dental practice-software vendor announces an "AI control tower" with agent inventory, approval gates and cross-practice benchmarks. That would mean the system-of-record owner in Cyclical's vertical has adopted the playbook.
