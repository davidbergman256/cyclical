# Analysis: ServiceTitan (Titan Intelligence, Atlas, Max, Virtual Agents, Homh)

ServiceTitan is the system of record (the software where every job, call, invoice and technician event is logged) for mid-to-large US home-service contractors: HVAC, plumbing, electrical and garage doors. In 2025–26 it turned that position into **Max**, a package of 30+ agents that share one "company brain", hand work to each other, and sit next to human customer-service reps (CSRs) and dispatchers in the same roles. It measures them on business outcomes such as booking rate, close rate, average ticket and "fully automated jobs". **Overlap: 6/10.** Max is the clearest live case of the A14 risk ("the system-of-record owner builds it"): it already has the whole graph of a contractor's work, outcome labels on every job, a shared context layer and an arbiter between agents. What it lacks is Cyclical's evaluation discipline (statistical graduation per case type, seeded audits, pooled randomized experiments) and any discovery of the graph from outside its own product. The most important lesson is that the incumbent won adoption by **selling the outcome as a package**: implementation is done for the customer, the first months are unbilled, and pricing runs about 2% of the contractor's revenue. It did not win by selling better evals. Its constraint is implementation capacity, not intelligence.

## What it actually is

**Company.** Public on NASDAQ (TTAN) since December 2024 (from memory). Fiscal-2027 guidance (year ending January 2027) is $1.139–1.144B of revenue and $152–154M of operating income, raised after the Q2 call in September 2026 [3][4]. Revenue grew ~21% year over year in Q2 FY27 [9]. At IPO it had roughly 8,000 active customers processing ~$62B of gross transaction volume (GTV: the dollar value of jobs invoiced through the platform) a year (from memory, S-1). Core pricing is per technician, quoted after a demo; third-party estimates run $245–500 per tech per month [10].

**Titan Intelligence (TI).** This is the AI layer, announced in 2023 (from memory), that the "Pro" products run on: Contact Center Pro (call handling and AI booking), Dispatch Pro (AI technician assignment), Marketing Pro, Sales Pro, Field Pro and Scheduling Pro (online booking) [2][11]. ServiceTitan claims early-access users of Contact Center Pro saw a 10% increase in raw booking rate (vendor claim) [11]. Its own guide puts the typical shop's inbound-call-to-booked-job rate at about 42% [12].

**Atlas.** Launched at Pantheon 2025 as a natural-language "sidekick" built on TI [13]. At Pantheon 2026 it was upgraded to a "chief of staff" agent that takes actions, delegates tasks to staff, and has its own mobile app that will replace the Office app [1][2]. Listed capabilities: query any report including custom ones, generate new data queries, analyze uploaded budgets and forecasts, watch KPIs (key performance indicators) and make recommendations unprompted, and **create workflow automations** [2][14].

**Max.** Max is a packaged offering: all the Pro products, 30+ agents across "18 key business drivers", and a coordination layer described as "one company brain, many specialized agents". It runs across demand (marketing, lead handling), field (dispatch, technician coaching) and office (billing, follow-up) [1][5][15]. CEO Ara Mahdessian's stated reason for the coordinator is that independent agents "create conflicts when each optimizes only for its own metric." The fix he named is "shared context, shared judgment, coordinated actions, arbitration between competing priorities and centralized supervision of agents," and "one agent can ask another to act" [5][15]. His keynote example: demand forecasting sees weak shoulder-season HVAC demand and asks the advertising agent for more leads. A text agent answers the inbound customer. Dispatch moves a lower-value appointment, with a credit and the customer's consent, to make room [5]. Customers can run ServiceTitan's agents, third-party agents, or agents they build on its "agentic operating system", all supervised from one console [1][15].

**Max traction (all management-reported).**
- Locations: just over 100 after fiscal Q1 2027, more than doubled again in Q2, target >700 by fiscal year-end [3][6].
- Automation: "across fully ramped Max customers, more than 10% of jobs are now fully automated on average." Every fully ramped customer runs at least one fully automated job type where "the only human intervention is the technician in the field" [6].
- Outcomes: booking rates 500+ basis points (bp, hundredths of a percentage point) above peers and close rates 1,000+ bp above, for a limited early-access cohort, from unaudited internal data [7]. One customer, Team Rooter, raised online-booking conversion from 61% to 66% [7]. The Max page claims a 6× faster booked-jobs growth rate and an 11% higher average ticket [10]. CTO Abhishek Mathur claimed some Pro/Max customers grew revenue twice as fast as non-Max peers [15].
- Constraint: demand for full Max deployments exceeds implementation capacity. Management plans modular "on-ramp" packages (for example demand only, or field ops only) and now sells Max to new logos (customers new to ServiceTitan) before they adopt the core product [3].
- Scope: residential in-home trades. The Pantheon 2026 press release says "all residential in-home contractors". The live blog says plumbing, heating, electrical and garage doors with 4+ technicians. Commercial and roofing are in a limited pilot [1][16].

**Pricing: is it outcome-based?** Partly, and indirectly. Verified pieces:
- Voice and text virtual agents are billed on consumption, "per call, per resolved outcome, or per text message" [8].
- Max subscription revenue per enrollee "roughly doubles once contracts fully ramp", with an average **platform earn rate just north of 2%** of the customer's GTV [8]. That is ServiceTitan's revenue as a share of the contractor's invoiced revenue. For comparison, core ServiceTitan earned roughly 1% (from memory).
- Max customers get an initial unbilled period and ramp over the first year. Onboarding fees are waived for existing customers moving to Max, which creates a $4–5M near-term revenue headwind [3][8].

I found no contract that ties the Max fee to booked jobs or revenue lift. An analyst asked on the Q4 FY26 call whether pricing would shift toward GTV and transactions and away from technician count, and I could not retrieve the answer [8]. So pricing is outcome-flavored: some agent usage is metered per resolved outcome, and total take scales with contractor revenue. It is not pay-for-performance. Probability Max has a true results-contingent price for most customers: ~15%.

**Homh.** A consumer demand platform (homeowner-facing) launched at Pantheon 2026 alongside Max [1][16]. I could not retrieve its mechanics. The strategic read (inference) is that ServiceTitan moves upstream from software to the channel that sends demand into its own contractors' booking agents. That closes the loop from lead to paid invoice inside one company.

**"Agents without ServiceTitan data are limited."** This is ServiceTitan's positioning, not a tested claim. In substance the keynote argued that fragmented point tools cause "wasted spend and poor coordination" [5], and that only a shared brain over the system of record can arbitrate between agents [15]. The structural fact behind it is real. A third-party voice agent (Avoca, Sameday) can book a job through the API, but it cannot see whether the technician sold a $12k system or whether the invoice was paid unless the system of record exposes that, and ServiceTitan now owns Contact Center Pro as the "recommended telecom" for Max [17].

**A1 and A7, where they are closest.** ServiceTitan already treats "who answered the call" as a swappable occupant of one role. The Voice Agent tags the jobs it books, CSR bookings and self-serve bookings land in the same job record, and downstream dispatch logic doesn't care which booked them [17]. That is Cyclical's interchangeable node, implemented as a tag on a shared record. "Fully automated job" is a per-job-type autonomy metric that matches Cyclical's "graduate per case type, not per node." The gap is the rule. Cyclical graduates a case type after n clean runs against real outcomes; ServiceTitan's implementation team flips it on when the contractor is comfortable (inference from the implementation-led rollout; no public threshold).

**A11, the gap that matters most.** ServiceTitan holds the pooled data Cyclical says it needs: thousands of contractors running the same call→book→dispatch→sell→invoice process with outcome labels on every run. But its own help center tells customers to judge Max against their own baseline, because "the right numbers depend on trade, lead sources, and goals" [18]. Its peer comparisons are observational, comparing a self-selected early cohort to non-Max peers [7], which is exactly the kind of evidence a randomized trial across hundreds of businesses would replace. Nothing I found suggests ServiceTitan runs randomized experiments across customers. Its incentive is to sell Max to everyone, not to hold out a control group.

**A12.** Mahdessian's framing ("independent agents create conflicts when each optimizes only for its own metric") is the same argument Cyclical makes with its downcoding claims-agent example, arrived at independently and shipped as a central arbiter. ServiceTitan's version is runtime arbitration between agents. Cyclical's is an eval-time guardrail metric. Both are needed, and ServiceTitan has the runtime half.

## Where they're ahead of Cyclical

- **Outcome labels for free.** Every booking links to the job, the sold estimate, the invoice and the payment in one database. Cyclical has to reconstruct this from logs and integrations, and the "who answers for the system node" problem (A4) is only a problem for someone who doesn't own the system.
- **Live autonomy at scale.** More than 10% of jobs fully automated across ramped customers, a few hundred locations live, 700+ targeted by January 2027 [3][6]. Cyclical has a simulator.
- **Packaging and pricing that make outcomes the product.** Unbilled ramp, waived onboarding, consumption billing "per resolved outcome" for agents, and revenue that doubles per customer once ramped (~2% of GTV) [3][8]. Contractors buy "more booked jobs", not "an eval loop".
- **Runtime multi-agent arbitration** with a shared brain, and an open slot for third-party agents [1][15]. This makes ServiceTitan the orchestration layer even for point-solution startups.
- **Demand channel (Homh).** Owning where leads come from lets the system run experiments on the front of the funnel that no back-office tool can.

## Where they're doing it differently (and whether their way is better)

- **Fixed ontology versus a discovered graph.** ServiceTitan's graph is its product's module map. That works because trades businesses already run ServiceTitan end to end, so the map matches reality. In dental, where the clinic's work spans the practice-management system, insurer portals, email, phones and accounting, a discovered graph is the more faithful model. Their way is better *inside a vertical with one dominant end-to-end system of record*; Cyclical's way is necessary where that system doesn't exist.
- **Implementation team versus statistical graduation.** ServiceTitan gets trust by having humans configure and ramp each customer over ~90 days, with a review template at Day 30 and Day 90 [18]. This is slow (capacity-bound [3]) but sells. Cyclical's statistical gates could make the ramp automatic and much cheaper per customer. That is the one place Cyclical's method is plainly better, and it matters to ServiceTitan's own constraint.
- **Per-customer baselines versus pooled experiments.** ServiceTitan evaluates each contractor against itself [18]. That is weaker evidence, but it is easier to explain to an owner. Cyclical should expect to need both.
- **Keep the CSRs versus pool the leftover humans.** ServiceTitan's agents escalate to each contractor's own CSRs and call-center manager [7]. Cyclical's pooled exception desk is a structurally different labor model. ServiceTitan could add it but hasn't.

## Feedback & reviews on these parts of the loop

**Independent evidence on Max and Atlas is close to nil.** My search results explicitly found "no independent evaluation of how well Atlas performs in practice". All Max performance figures are company-reported, from an early-access cohort, and unaudited [7]. I could not retrieve G2, Capterra or Reddit threads in this session (blocked fetches, exhausted search budget). That gap should be filled before relying on adoption claims.

- **Outcomes (A5/A11), vendor-sourced.** "Call booking rates more than 500 basis points above peers, close rates more than 1,000 basis points above," from "internal platform data for a limited early-access cohort" [7]. Selection bias is obvious: early-access customers are the most engaged contractors.
- **Autonomy (A7), vendor-sourced, earnings call.** "More than 10% of jobs are now fully automated on average" across fully ramped customers [6]. "Fully ramped" excludes the newest and hardest customers.
- **Adoption and constraint, earnings call.** Demand for full deployments "exceeds implementation capacity", hence modular on-ramps [3]. This is the strongest signal in the record: the bottleneck is human implementation labor, not model quality.
- **Financial skeptic signal.** An analyst asked on the Q4 FY26 call whether pricing should move from technician count to GTV and transactions [8]. Investors see that AI agents erode per-seat pricing when they reduce CSR and dispatcher headcount.
- **Core product sentiment (from memory, not re-verified this session).** ServiceTitan scores roughly 4.4–4.5/5 on G2 across several hundred reviews. Recurring complaints are price, long contracts, a steep learning curve and heavy implementation. On r/HVAC and r/smallbusiness it is often described as built for larger shops and expensive for under ~5 techs. This matches Max's 4+ tech eligibility floor [16].
- **AI phone answering in trades (from memory).** Contractor forum sentiment on AI receptionists is mixed. Owners like after-hours capture but worry about emergency calls and customers hanging up on a bot. That is the same case-type split (routine booking versus emergency) that Cyclical's per-case-type graduation handles.

## Top-level: why they chose this path, and what to learn

**Starting asset and constraints.** ServiceTitan began (2012) as the system of record for larger residential contractors, monetized per technician, and grew by adding "Pro" modules on top of the same job record. By 2025 it had the job, call, dispatch and invoice history of thousands of contractors and ~$60B+ of GTV (from memory). Two constraints shaped Max. First, AI that answers calls and dispatches reduces the office headcount, and eventually the seat counts, that per-seat pricing implicitly relies on. ServiceTitan's answer is to reprice toward a share of contractor revenue (~2% earn rate) plus per-outcome agent billing [8]. Second, contractors don't trust a bot with their phones by default. ServiceTitan's answer is people: an implementation team, a 90-day ROI review [18], waived fees and an unbilled ramp [3]. The orchestrator ("company brain") exists because ServiceTitan already sold 8+ separate Pro products that pulled against each other, and arbitration was the cheapest way to make them one product.

**What this means for Cyclical's thesis.** In trades, the thesis is already half-taken. The system-of-record owner has the graph, outcome labels, shared context, multi-agent arbitration, per-job-type autonomy, and outcome-flavored pricing. It is not running Cyclical's eval science: no statistical graduation, no seeded audits, no pooled randomized trials, no pooled exception desk. But its implementation-led model works well enough to have more demand than it can serve. Cyclical is a **superset on evaluation method and a subset on everything ServiceTitan owns** (data, channel, customers). Entering trades would be pointless. The question is whether dental's system-of-record vendors (Henry Schein One/Dentrix, Patterson/Eaglesoft, Planet DDS, Curve, Open Dental) can do what ServiceTitan did. They are older, more fragmented and less AI-forward (from memory), which is the main reason dental is still open.

**Copy.**
1. Sell the outcome, not the loop. Use an unbilled ramp and per-resolved-outcome agent pricing that rises to a share of the business's revenue (for dental, a share of collections).
2. Make the human/agent swap a tag on a shared record, so every outcome can be split by who did the step.
3. Use "fully automated case" (no human but the clinician) as the headline metric. It is legible to owners and maps exactly onto per-case-type graduation.
4. Build runtime arbitration as well as eval-time guardrails.

**Avoid.** Implementation-team-led ramps. They are ServiceTitan's binding constraint [3], and Cyclical's statistical graduation is the automated replacement. Avoid fixed module ontologies in a vertical where work spans many systems.

**Exploit.**
1. ServiceTitan evaluates customers against their own baselines and self-selected peer cohorts [7][18]. Pooled randomized evidence ("removing the second front-desk role didn't hurt collections across 200 clinics") is something an incumbent that sells to everyone is poorly incentivized to produce.
2. Its agents escalate to each customer's own staff. A pooled exception desk is a cost structure ServiceTitan's per-customer model doesn't have.
3. In dental, process mining across the practice-management system plus insurer portals and email is the way to get ServiceTitan-grade outcome labels without owning the system of record.

**Threat level to Cyclical: high in trades (do not enter), medium in dental.** These observations would show the threat rising:
- (a) A dental practice-management vendor announces a Max-style package: multi-agent, unbilled ramp, priced on a percentage of collections.
- (b) ServiceTitan or a peer publishes *randomized* cross-customer results or pooled graduation thresholds, which would mean it has adopted Cyclical's method.
- (c) ServiceTitan's Max locations pass ~2,000 with implementation no longer the stated bottleneck, which would mean it has automated the ramp.

Probability (a) happens with a credible product within 24 months: ~40%.
