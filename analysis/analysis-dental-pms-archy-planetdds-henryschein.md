# Analysis: Dental practice-management vendors building agents (Archy, Planet DDS, Henry Schein One, and the rest of the PMS field)

These are the companies whose databases hold the answer keys Cyclical wants to grade against: appointments kept, claims paid, ERA lines (electronic remittance advice, the insurer's machine-readable payment statement). All three leaders now ship task agents inside the system of record. None ships Cyclical's loop: no process graph, no human-vs-agent outcome grading, no graduation rule per case type, and no randomized test of whether a role can be removed. **Overlap 4/10.** They cover A3, A10 and the data half of A11 better than Cyclical can as an outsider, and A1, A5–A9 and A12 not at all. The threat is still the highest in the dental field, because Cyclical needs their data to run at all and Henry Schein One has a ten-year record of charging for that access and suing over it. The most important lesson: Cyclical's unresolved A4 question (who answers a permission request sent to the practice-software node?) is already answered in dental, and the answer is "the PMS vendor, commercially." Which PMS Cyclical builds on first is therefore a strategic decision, not an integration detail.

## What it actually is

**Market structure.** There is no audited market-share data. Henry Schein One says more than 48,000 US practices run Dentrix or Dentrix Ascend [vendor claim, 5]. Patterson reports 38,000+ dental software customers over 25 years, mostly on Eaglesoft [5]. The most-cited independent figure is a 2019 ADA / Maryland Health Care Commission survey: Dentrix 36% of practices, Eaglesoft 24% [5]. A May 2024 CIRO analysis of about 40,500 practices puts Dentrix at 32.5% of single-location offices but only 22.8% of multi-location ones (Eaglesoft 20.8% vs 11.8%, Open Dental 15.9% vs 12.4%) [5]. The share of multi-location practices those three lose goes mostly to cloud enterprise systems, and Denticon is the largest of them. A DSO (dental service organization) is a company that runs the business side (billing, insurance, phones, HR) for many dental offices. Planet DDS says more than half of the top 60 North American DSOs use its products [vendor claim, 13]. The US has roughly 200,000 dentists in about 130,000 practice locations (from memory).

**Archy** raised a $50M Series C on Sept 11 2026, led by JMI Equity with TCV, Bessemer and CRV; total funding is about $97M, and the round came less than a year after a $20M Series B in Oct 2025 [1, 15]. CEO Jonathan Rat. Archy says it processes $300M+ a year in payments [vendor, 15] and earlier claimed customers save "80 hours a month" [vendor, 15]. A third-party profile cites 3M+ claims created [15]. Integrated payments are part of the business model, so Archy earns a cut of every dollar collected (from memory). That gives it a direct financial reason to make the Revenue agent collect more.

**Henry Schein One** is Henry Schein's dental-software arm (originally a 2019 joint venture with Internet Brands, from memory). In 2026 it repositioned Dentrix Ascend as the DSO product: new packages in March 2026, with a "Pro" tier that adds Eligibility Pro checks and multi-location management [7, 8]. Its "autonomous RCM" announcement (RCM, revenue cycle management, is everything from eligibility check to final payment) frames Eligibility Pro as the template it will extend to claims, ERA reconciliation and patient collections, "with staff getting involved only when a claim can't go out the door on its own" [7]. The April 2026 MCP announcement (MCP, Model Context Protocol, is Anthropic's open standard for letting AI agents call a system's data and actions) is the first sign that Henry Schein One will expose Ascend data to agents it did not write. Only the read-only "Ask" tier was in preview, though, and "Build" had no date [4].

**Planet DDS** sells to DSOs. Its July 2026 announcement "declared the practice management era over" and launched DentalOS, which unifies Denticon, Cloud 9 and Apteryx on one data layer with single sign-on, central user management and an agentic marketplace, reportedly at no extra cost to existing customers [2]. Denticon ARR growth was 24.3% year over year [vendor, 13]. Recent wins include Smile Partners USA (120 locations, Aug 2026), Jefferson Dental (60 locations) and Mosaic Dental Collective (55+) [13]. It publishes an annual "Dental Industry Deep Dive" benchmarking DSO performance from its customer data (May 2026: "a widening performance gap across the DSO industry") [14]. That report shows Planet DDS already pools outcome data across businesses. Today it does so for marketing and benchmarks, not to train agents.

The prose below covers only the aspects where they differ in a way that matters.

**A4: the system node answers commercially.** thinking.md asks who answers a permission request sent to the practice software. In dental the answer exists: the PMS vendor, through a contract. Dentrix's API Exchange charges $5,000 for read and $5,000 for write setup plus a monthly royalty per API category, with "no start-up pricing and no access before joining" [9]. A secondary source reports the Ascend API at $5,000 setup plus $47 per Ascend location per month, with overages billed to the integrator [10]. Henry Schein One's security FAQ says it is making changes "that may affect how certain third-party vendors interact with Dentrix — specifically those not using our approved API program" [11]. So the graph's system node has an owner, and its rules go beyond Cyclical's A4 classes (who asks, data class, task type). The owner can also charge per location and revoke access. For Cyclical's permission design, "the system node's answers are set by the API contract, and the practice owner approves inside that envelope" is a cleaner rule than appointing a "responsible human" for each system. The tidiest version is a third class of node, *external authority*, whose grants come from contracts, not requests.

**A5 and A11: they hold the answer keys and the pool but do not run Cyclical's experiments.** HSO ran 191M eligibility checks in 2025 [7]. Planet DDS has schedule and claims data for over half of the top 60 DSOs and already publishes cross-DSO performance comparisons [13, 14]. Every input Cyclical's pooled outcome tests need is already in their warehouses. What they lack is the experimental design: outcome-graded shadow runs, seeded-error checkoffs, and above all randomized trials across hundreds of offices on whether a role can be removed. They have not built these because they sell software seats to the offices whose roles would be removed (see Top-level).

**A8: they start at the other end.** A PMS is decades of deterministic rules: fee schedules, insurance plan tables, claim scrubbers, auto-posting of ERA files. HSO's eligibility agent sits on top of EDI 270/271 transactions (the standard electronic eligibility request and response); Ascend's own guide notes that "responses returned at the Essentials level (EDI) do not count as Pro responses" [7]. Cyclical's level-3 step (compile the agent into code once a rule reproduces it) is the move the PMS vendors made long ago without agents. So the code layer Cyclical would generate largely already exists inside the PMS. Cyclical's compiled-code level is worth less in dental than thinking.md implies, and its agent levels 1–2 matter more for the unstructured work: phone calls, denials, insurer portals, patient texts.

## Where they're ahead of Cyclical

- **Distribution and data custody.** Tens of thousands of practices already run their software, and the agents ship as a tier upgrade. Henry Schein One sells Eligibility Pro inside Ascend Pro [7]; Planet DDS bundles DentalOS at no added cost [2]. Cyclical has to win each office's trust; they win the upgrade.
- **Zero-UI agent placement (A3).** An agent inside the PMS changes the schedule or claim directly. Cyclical's "draft in the email folder" pattern is weaker for the 12 of 14 handoffs that realizations.md says end in the practice software. Those drafts would have to be written *into* the PMS, which needs write access, the exact thing Dentrix charges $5,000 for.
- **Outcome labels for free (A5, A11 data).** Claim adjudication, ERA posting and appointment status sit in their tables already. Archy goes further by processing the payment itself, so it sees the bank-level outcome too [15].
- **DSO multi-location context (A10, A11).** Planet DDS's single data layer across DSO locations is the human-DSO pooling that realizations.md cites as proof of the economics, already in software form for DSOs.

## Where they're doing it differently (and whether their way is better)

**Agent per module vs graph.** The PMS vendors put one agent on each module they own: eligibility, confirmations, claims, notes. This is the "market of single-task agents" realizations.md §6 calls crowded, except that here it comes from the system-of-record owner, so it pushes standalone point vendors aside. Their way is better for shipping and selling. It is worse for the one thing Cyclical claims only it can do: testing structural changes, such as removing the treatment coordinator or merging verification into scheduling. Removing a role means selling one fewer seat, so they will avoid that test.

**Platform/marketplace vs orchestrator (A9).** HSO's MCP "Build" tier and Planet DDS's agentic marketplace leave composition to the customer and to partners. That makes the PMS a place where Cyclical could plug in. It also means a future "Orchestrate" tier could be the vendor's own orchestrator, with partner agents as interchangeable node occupants. If HSO's "Orchestrate" ever routes work between its own agents and partner agents and grades them, that would be A1 + A9 inside the system of record.

**Locked core, open edge (A13).** The vendors are model-agnostic where it helps them (MCP lets any model *call* Ascend) and locked where it protects them (the data). Cyclical's model-agnosticism protects against model suppliers. The bigger dependency risk in dental is the data supplier, and model-agnosticism does nothing about that.

**Become the system (Archy) vs sit on top.** Archy took the path realizations.md §6 names ("or become that system"): it replaced the PMS so it would own the event log, the payment rail and the answer keys. It got to 1,000 practices in about five years (founded ~2020–21, from memory) and $97M raised [1, 15]. That suggests an AI-native PMS can win share, but slowly. At 1,000 practices it has well under 1% of the market and fewer offices than Cyclical's 10,000-clinic model assumes.

## Feedback & reviews on these parts of the loop

**Independent evidence is thin, and almost none of it is about agent outcomes.** No independent measurement of collections, claim acceptance or show rate exists for any PMS agent I found. Every outcome figure is a vendor claim: Archy's "80 hours/month saved", HSO's "cut patient processing time in half" (one quoted business manager on the Eligibility Pro product page), and the trade-press "up to 30% fewer no-shows" attributed to Planet DDS's agents [7, 15, 2]. Planet DDS's own release does not state the 30% figure [2].

**Archy (independent, small n).** Capterra 4.7 (n=6), G2 4.1 (n=7), Apple App Store 4.9 (n=79) [16]. The most repeated complaint concerns the money side, exactly where Archy Revenue operates. A G2 reviewer: "Ledger works different then most dental software. It takes some time to understand it." Another dislikes "the ledger and invoices" but likes scheduling [16]. A Software Advice reviewer had trouble migrating records, "especially x-rays" [16]. A G2 reviewer cites the inability to create custom recalls [16]. Migration pain is the cost of the become-the-system path: each practice Archy wins has to go through a records migration first.

**Henry Schein One on third-party access (independent press and court filings).**
- *Vyne Dental lawsuits (Oct 2025 onward).* HSO sued Vyne in Utah federal court on Oct 3 2025, alleging that former HSO executives helped Vyne "deploy malicious code to intercept web traffic, circumvent security measures and redirect dental claims to Vyne's servers" [17]. Vyne countersued in Maryland under the 21st Century Cures Act's information-blocking rules, alleging that HSO "launched an aggressive campaign to interfere with Vyne and the company's customers" and tried to block mutual customers from sending Dentrix records to Vyne Trellis [17, 18]. Final arguments on dueling preliminary-injunction motions were heard March 18 (2026) [18]. I could not find the ruling. DentistryIQ framed the dispute as a question of "who controls patient data" [18].
- *2016 FTC order.* The FTC finalized an order against Henry Schein Practice Solutions for misleading claims that Dentrix G5 encrypted patient data [19] ($250,000 settlement, from memory). It shows that HSO's "security" rationale for controlling access has been contested before.
- *Health API Guy* (Brendan Keeler, a health-interoperability analyst) wrote a critical post on Henry Schein's API posture ("Henry Scheinfreude") [20]; I could not read it in this run.
- An API-review site gives Dentrix's API "Grade F" and says access requires "application, use-case review, security review, and signed API Agreement before any keys are issued, no self-serve" [secondary, 21].
- AI-receptionist vendors report connecting to Dentrix through intermediaries such as NexHealth, which already hold Dentrix access and add another party to the patient-data chain [10].

**Henry Schein One on its own agents (vendor-sourced).** Eligibility Pro won Gold in the 2026 Dental Innovator Awards (vendor claim; the award listing was not found) [7]. The claims, ERA reconciliation and collections agents are roadmap, not shipped [7].

**Planet DDS (vendor-sourced, plus a technology columnist).** Dentistry technology blogger John Flucke covered the DentalOS launch favorably [2]. No independent reviews of the Confirmation, Scheduling or Recall agents were found in this run. The waitlist launch in February and the "expanded AI agent suite" press release in August 2026 [22] suggest roughly a six-month path from early access to wider release.

## Top-level: why they chose this path, and what to learn

**Starting assets and constraints.** Each vendor's position follows from what it started with.
- **Henry Schein One** owns the largest installed base, much of it on a desktop product (Dentrix G-series) where a database-level API is a toll booth. API royalties and data-access control earn money directly and protect HSO's adjacent businesses: eClaims, payments, patient communication. Its parent sells supplies to the same practices. Its agents are tier upgrades that raise revenue per seat, and its natural move is to tax or acquire third-party agents, not to host them openly. The MCP announcement is a hedge: open enough to stay relevant in an agent world, gated enough to keep the toll (Build tier undated).
- **Planet DDS** sells to DSOs, which buy integrations and run many vendors, so openness (100+ partners) is how it wins against Dentrix in the multi-location segment. Its agents target what a DSO's central office measures: confirmations, scheduling, recall, then RCM.
- **Archy** started with no installed base and no data, so it had to become the system of record before any agent could see an outcome. Its payments revenue makes collections its own top-line metric.

**What none of them will do.** They sell per location and per seat to the practices whose staff an agent would replace. Randomized role-removal trials, a pooled exception desk that replaces in-office billing staff, and grading agents against staff by outcome all threaten their customers' payroll politics and their own seat counts. This is the opening Cyclical's thesis relies on. A DSO, though, is a customer that *wants* fewer front-office FTEs (full-time equivalents) per location. Planet DDS serves DSOs and already benchmarks them against each other [14], so it has both the incentive and the data to move first if any PMS vendor does.

**Clone, subset, superset or orthogonal?** Cyclical is a **superset in method and a dependent in data**. It adds the graph, the outcome-graded eval stages, graduation and pooled experiments, none of which these vendors ship. It cannot run any of them without read and ideally write access to their databases. The overlap is real on A3, A10 and the data half of A11, where they are structurally better positioned.

**Copy:**
- Agents that act inside the PMS rather than drafting beside it. For the 12 of 14 handoffs that end in the PMS, Cyclical's "sending = approval" needs a PMS equivalent: a pending change the human commits.
- Planet DDS's play of publishing benchmarks from pooled data. A public "show-rate and collections benchmark by insurer × procedure" both recruits clinics into the pool and demonstrates the moat.
- Archy's alignment of revenue with outcomes (a share of collections), which doubles as the A12 business-level guardrail.

**Avoid:**
- Building first on Dentrix G-series desktop through unofficial database access. The Vyne litigation shows HSO will sue [17]. The fees ($10,000 setup for read and write plus royalties [9]) are payable but give HSO a kill switch.
- Competing as yet another single-task agent (eligibility, confirmations). The PMS vendors now ship those as bundled tier features.

**Exploit:**
- Start on the open and DSO-friendly systems: Open Dental (published API), Denticon (100+ partners, DSO buyers, marketplace), and Dentrix Ascend via MCP once Orchestrate or Build opens. Together these cover most multi-location practices, which is where Cyclical's DSO-shaped economics sit.
- Pitch Cyclical to DSOs as what their PMS vendor will not offer: proof, from randomized trials across offices, of which front-office roles can be removed. Planet DDS's marketplace is a distribution channel for exactly that.
- Use the Cures Act information-blocking argument (Vyne's theory [17]) as leverage if access is refused. Practices own their patients' data. Probability that the Vyne case produces a ruling limiting HSO's ability to block practice-authorized vendors: about 40% (rough guess; outcome not found).

**Threat level: high, and it rises with any of these observations:**
1. HSO opens Ascend MCP "Orchestrate"/"Build" generally *and* adds per-agent outcome dashboards (collections or acceptance by agent vs staff). That would be A5 inside the system of record.
2. Planet DDS's DentalOS publishes a cross-DSO A/B result for an agent, such as confirmation-text variants tested across customers. That would be A11 experiments with the largest DSO pool.
3. Any of the three prices an agent on outcomes (per kept appointment, % of collections) rather than per seat. Archy is closest.
4. A PMS vendor changes its API terms to exclude "AI agents that perform staff functions," or starts per-call AI pricing. This one threatens access, not features.
5. Archy passes about 5,000 practices or starts selling to DSOs. At that point an AI-native PMS has enough volume to run pooled experiments itself.
