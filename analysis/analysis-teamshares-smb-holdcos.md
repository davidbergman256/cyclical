# Analysis: Teamshares and small-business holding companies (incl. 2025–26 "AI-native" roll-ups)

Teamshares (Nasdaq: TMS since June 2026) owns about 92 small businesses in 40+ industries. It centralizes the parts of their work that look the same in every industry: the books, finance, president recruiting and training, and an employee-ownership app. It publishes no evidence that the platform improves operations: organic revenue growth was 3.6% in Q3 2026, and management says AI helps mainly with "financial accounting" and building software faster. The 2025–26 AI-native roll-ups (AGI, COVU and Equal Parts in insurance agencies, Numica and Thrive's Crete in accounting, Long Lake in HOA management, Dwelly in UK property management) all chose **one vertical**. That is market evidence for realizations.md's "pick one vertical" claim, with one refinement. Teamshares shows that pooling works across industries only for processes that are the same in every industry (the general ledger, accounts payable, payroll). Overlap score: **Teamshares 3/10** (it owns many businesses and has a shared back office, but no process graph, no agent evals and no outcome pooling per process). **AI-native single-vertical holdcos 5/10** (they run agents across many owned businesses in one process family, which is Cyclical's A11 economics bought through ownership instead of sold as software). The most important lesson: **pool by process, not by industry.** The single-vertical AI holdco is both Cyclical's most dangerous competitor and its best possible customer.

## What it actually is

**Teamshares**
- **Model.** It buys profitable, owner-run small businesses from retiring owners, at $0.5–5M of EBITDA (some sources say up to $10M). It puts in a hired "president", plugs the business into the Teamshares platform, centralizes certain functions, and gives employees stock. Employees start with 10% and the target is 80% employee ownership within about 20 years [1][2][8]. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a rough measure of operating cash profit.
- **Scale.** About 92 companies, about $490M consolidated revenue, 40+ industries in 30 states [1][3]. The March 2026 investor-day summary gave about $59M of operating (subsidiary) EBITDA, roughly a 12% margin, and a separate "$19M corporate EBITDA" figure whose sign the summaries leave unclear [3].
- **Listing.** It went public through a merger with Live Oak Acquisition Corp. V (a SPAC, a listed shell company that merges with a private one), closing June 18, 2026. Implied valuation was about $746M and enterprise value about $825M, with a $126M PIPE (private investment alongside the merger) led by T. Rowe Price and $132.4M of gross proceeds [4][5][6].
- **Q3 2026** (reported Nov 16) [7][9][10]:
  - Revenue was $147.9M, up 24% year over year but only **3.6% organic** (excluding acquisitions), and about 17% below the ~$177–179M consensus.
  - Adjusted EBITDA was $4.9M. Pro forma adjusted EBITDA for the last twelve months was $23.1M at Sep 30, against a year-end target of about $60M.
  - It made 10 acquisitions year to date, adding about $20M of acquired EBITDA.
  - It issued $225M of perpetual preferred stock.
  - The stock fell 14% to $4.50, then to $4.19 after hours. The SPAC price was $10.
- **Debt.** About $187.8M of debt matures within 12 months, including about $153.4M on an i80 facility due December 2026 [6].
- **The platform:**
  - **TeamsharesOS.** An app for employee owners: company financials, ownership education, "in-platform discussion questions" [8].
  - **Central accounting.** Job listings describe a "primary general ledger accountant for acquired companies undergoing transition" [8].
  - **Presidents.** Recruited, often from large companies or MBA programs (from memory), then put through "an intensive training program… ongoing oversight, expert support" [8][12].
  - **Shared services and playbooks.** The 10-Q says success "depends largely on expanding the platform without proportionately increasing its corporate cost structure" [1].
- **AI posture (Q3 call).** The businesses are "durable, evergreen" and face little AI revenue risk. AI's internal benefit is "financial accounting" and "how quickly it can build software" [10][11]. The company publishes no per-process metrics: no close times, collection rates, or headcount per dollar of revenue.

**Peers (traditional)**
- **Enduring Ventures.** Founded 2019 by Xavier Helgesen (Better World Books, Zola Electric) and Sieva Kozinsky. A Berkshire-style permanent holdco buying 51–100% of cash-flow-positive SMBs [13][14]. No shared-services software platform turned up in search.
- **Bluestone.** Search returned nothing usable, so it is not assessed.
- **Search-fund holdcos** (one operator buys one business, sometimes several). These are generally decentralized (from memory).
- **Constellation Software.** The far more successful decentralized analog: hundreds of vertical-software businesses benchmarked against each other inside each vertical, deliberately not run on a shared ops stack (from memory). It is the cleanest historical test of "pool within a vertical".

**The 2025–26 AI-native holdcos.** All are single-vertical.

No AI-native holdco in HVAC, plumbing, landscaping, dental or vet turned up in searches, and the caritas.ventures market map lists the category broadly [22]. Dental is already rolled up by human DSOs (dental service organizations, which pool billing, insurance checks, phones and recall for hundreds of offices). An "AI-native DSO" is the scenario to watch.

**A11 is where it matters.** Teamshares is a natural experiment on realizations.md's "pick one vertical" claim, and the result is mixed. Its central functions are the ones that are identical in an HVAC firm and a pharmacy: closing the books, payroll, benefits, insurance buying, banking. Its stated AI win is "financial accounting", the most horizontal process there is [10]. It publishes nothing showing that the platform improves the core operations of each business. Organic growth was 3.6% in a 24% headline quarter [9], and Q3 revenue missed consensus by about 17%. That is what you would expect if pooling across 40 industries gives a scale advantage on shared back-office work and almost nothing on the front-office processes (claims, scheduling, quoting) that drive revenue. Cyclical's dental thesis targets exactly those front-office processes: insurer × procedure claims, confirmation texts, recall. So diversity across industries does not kill pooling. It caps pooling at the horizontal processes. Restated, the claim should be: *pool by process; vertical is a proxy for having enough of the same process.* Dental clears that bar for the front office, and a horizontal accounting product could clear it across industries. Rough probability that this reading is right: 70%. The alternative is that Teamshares' platform does improve operations but it does not disclose it.

**A4 and the ownership shortcut.** A holdco never asks staff for permission, never has to prove value before getting access, and can replace the practice software. This removes three of Cyclical's hardest problems: adoption, permissions, and system-of-record dependence. The AI-native roll-ups exist because buying the business is a faster route to the data and to the authority to remove a role than selling software into it. The randomized "can this role be removed" test that realizations.md says needs hundreds of businesses can be run by a holdco by decree.

## Where they're ahead of Cyclical

- **A11 exception desk exists and runs.** Teamshares already operates a central finance and accounting team serving about 92 businesses [1][8], the human version of Cyclical's ~0.1 FTE/clinic desk. Its cost structure, which the 10-Q says must grow slower than the portfolio [1], is a real-world benchmark Cyclical lacks.
- **Authority to change the org.** A holdco can remove a role, merge functions or swap software without anyone's consent. Cyclical's orchestrator (A9) can only *propose*.
- **Distribution into businesses without a successor.** About half of US small-business owners are near retirement (from memory; the "silver tsunami" figure is commonly cited as ~2.9M businesses). Teamshares' pipeline is a channel to businesses that never buy SaaS.
- **Capital and scale already assembled.** About $490M of revenue and public-market access (at a falling price). Among AI holdcos, AGI ($70M) and Thrive (~$2B) can buy a vertical's worth of outcome data faster than Cyclical can accumulate it customer by customer.

## Where they're doing it differently (and whether their way is better)

- **Owning instead of selling.** Owning removes consent friction but adds capital cost and integration risk. Teamshares shows the bill:
  - $225M of perpetual preferred stock;
  - $153M of debt due in December 2026;
  - shares at roughly 45% of the $10 SPAC price [6][7];
  - a Chartmill headline about the "costly capital behind acquisition engine" [7].

  The model's binding constraint is the **cost of capital**, not intelligence or data. For Cyclical, ownership is better only if the per-unit uplift from agents is large and proven. Teamshares has not shown that uplift.
- **Breadth over depth.** Teamshares chose 40+ industries because the acquisition funnel is the product: retiring owners exist in every industry, and the ownership story is industry-neutral. That choice caps operational learning. Its way is better for deal flow and worse for process improvement, and the 3.6% organic growth figure is consistent with that.
- **A fixed human/agent split.** AGI puts "a human service model" in front and AI agents in back [15]. That is a static decision, not Cyclical's per-case-type graduation (A7). Cyclical's way is better on paper because it would find the case types where an agent is already good enough in front. AGI's way is cheaper to explain to sellers and staff.
- **Migrate rather than observe.** Numica moves acquired books onto its own system [19]. Teamshares moves subsidiaries onto the central GL. When you own the business, migration beats process mining. Cyclical's "no new box" stance (A2) matters only for businesses it does not own.

**AI-native holdcos (mostly vendor claims)**
- AGI's results rest on "a pilot with 10 partner agencies and one completed acquisition" [15][16].

## Top-level: why they chose this path, and what to learn

**Teamshares' starting asset** was a sourcing story (employee ownership for retiring owners) plus financing. Its customer is the seller, not the operator. Its incentive is to maximize acquired EBITDA per dollar of capital, which rewards breadth across industries and a platform that centralizes only what is cheap to standardize: books, payroll, banking, president training. The constraint that shaped everything is capital. The platform has to justify itself as overhead that grows slower than the portfolio [1], not as an engine for improving operations. Its AI remarks fit that: AI is a back-office cost-saver, and the businesses are "AI-proof" [10].

**AI-native holdcos** start from the opposite asset, a belief that agents can run a vertical's back office. They pick one vertical, and so far mostly insurance agencies and accounting firms, whose work is documents, rules and payment outcomes, close to Cyclical's dental front office. They are Cyclical's thesis with ownership bolted on.

**Copy**
- The central desk's economics. Get Teamshares' central finance headcount per subsidiary from the 10-Q or S-4 when the network allows; it is a real benchmark for A11's ~0.1 FTE/clinic.
- The ownership insight as a *channel*: sell Cyclical to DSOs and AI-native holdcos. They already own hundreds of same-process units, can authorize role-removal experiments, and need a measurement system (A5–A7) that none of them shows publicly.

**Avoid**
- Breadth across industries for front-office pooling. Teamshares is the cautionary case: 92 businesses and 40 industries produce scale on the GL and little visible operating lift.
- Financing a roll-up before the per-unit uplift is proven. The $225M preferred and $153M maturity show what happens when acquisition pace runs ahead of operating proof.

**Exploit**
- None of these firms publishes outcome-graded, per-case-type agent evals. A holdco buying agents needs exactly the graduation evidence of A5–A7 to know where it can cut people without hurting collections (A12). Cyclical can be their eval and orchestration layer.
- Reframe "pick one vertical" as "pick one process family." Horizontal processes such as AP/AR and month-end close can be pooled across Teamshares-style portfolios as a second product.

**Clone, subset, superset or orthogonal?** Cyclical is orthogonal to Teamshares: it does not own businesses, and Teamshares has none of the eval loop. It is a near-superset of the AI-native holdcos on method but a subset on authority. Their ownership gives them the data access and role-removal power Cyclical must earn.

**Threat level.** Teamshares: low. AI-native single-vertical holdcos: medium and rising. The signal that would tell David the threat is up: an AI-native acquirer in dental (or a large DSO) publishing per-location front-office headcount falling, or per-task autonomy rates, across 100+ locations. That would mean someone is accumulating the pooled outcome history by ownership. A second signal: AGI or COVU disclosing agency-level metrics such as policies serviced per employee or E&O claim rates (errors-and-omissions insurance claims against the agency) after 12 months.
